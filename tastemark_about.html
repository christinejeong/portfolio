Overview

Tastemark is a working web tool built on one belief: in the AI era, generating design is cheap — choosing well, and knowing why, is the job. AI produces the variants; the designer curates. Tastemark records every fork, every rejection, and every stated reason, then turns the whole session into a shareable decision artifact.

The pitch in one line: “GitHub commit history, but for design decisions.” Code has version control that proves how a developer thinks. Design has only final artifacts — the thinking is invisible. Tastemark makes it visible, recordable, and shareable.

Background

Designers are watching AI generate in seconds what used to take a week — and portfolios full of polished finals no longer prove much, because a model can produce polished finals too.

But hiring hasn't changed what it actually tests: can you defend a decision? Interviews probe the “why” behind every screen. The skill that survives AI is judgment — taste with reasons attached. Nothing in a designer's toolkit captures that skill as evidence.

The Problem

How might we make design judgment — not just design output — visible and provable?

  • Invisible thinking → the reasoning behind a design exists only in the designer's head, or in a meeting that wasn't recorded.
  • Winner-only portfolios → finals show what was chosen, never what was killed or why — which is where the judgment actually lives.
  • Unstructured critique → “I just like this one better” is where taste goes to die; without a forcing function, rationale never gets written down.

The Solution

A curation loop with a rule: you cannot advance without stating why.

  • Brief → 6 directions: a parametric engine (or Claude, with your own API key) generates six genuinely different live-rendered hero designs.
  • Forced rationale: the winner needs a tag plus one honest sentence; every kill needs a one-click reason. The friction is the product.
  • Evolve: your pick spawns the next generation — four refinements, two wildcards — for up to four rounds.
  • The artifact: an SVG decision tree of every fork, a decision log, and a rejection gallery — because what you killed says more than what you kept.
  • AI disagrees ⚡: the engine argues for one of your kills, and your rebuttal goes on the record — the exact pressure a design interview applies.
  • Share by URL: the entire session is encoded into the link itself. No account, no server, no database.

Design Decisions

Six variants, not a hundred
Choice overload kills curation. Small batches with “evolve this direction” beat an infinite wall of options — the loop stays a conversation, not a chore.

Calibrated friction
Early tests of the idea made every decision demand a full sentence — exhausting by round three. The final rule: one sentence for the winner, one click for each kill. Rationale everywhere, fatigue nowhere.

HTML/CSS variants instead of image generation
Generated code renders live, costs almost nothing, and behaves like a design tool rather than an AI art toy. Every variant is a real page in a sandboxed iframe.

Zero-backend architecture
The whole product is one HTML file. Sessions persist locally and share links carry the full session, compressed into the URL — an engineering constraint turned into a feature.

Takeaways

  • Constraints can be the concept: “no server” produced the share-by-URL design, which became a differentiator.
  • Friction isn't always the enemy — the forced “why” is the entire value of the product, so the UX job was calibrating it, not removing it.
  • Building the tool sharpened the thesis it argues: the artifact that matters isn't the design — it's the reasoning.
